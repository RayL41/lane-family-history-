Transcription of the Last Will and Testament of Michael Brooker, Labourer.

Film Location: East Sussex Record Office, Lewes.

Will Dated: 22nd March 1805 and Proved at South Malling on the 19th June 1806. Ref. No. SM D11/58

Michael Brooker was baptised at Framfield on the 15th April 1716, the son of Edward Brooker and Elizabeth, nee Newnham.

His burial took place at Framfield on the 23rd September 1805, when his age is given in the register as 90 years)

In the name of God Amen, I Michael Brooker of the Parish of Framfield in the County of Sussex, Labourer, being of a sound and disposing mind, memory and understanding, thanks be given to Almighty God for the same, and considering the certainty of death and the uncertainty of the hour thereof, do make my last Will and Testament in manner following, that is to say –

First I command my soul into the hands of Almighty God my Creator hoping through the interception of my Lord and Saviour, Jesus Christ, to receive pardon and remission of all my sins. My body I commit to the earth to be decently interred at the discretion, and by the direction, of my Executors, hereinafter named. And as to such worldly estate with which it hath pleased God to bless me, I dispose thereof as follows.

First I direct that all my just debts and funeral expenses be fully paid and discharged. Also I give, devise and bequeath unto my dear and loving wife, if it should so happen that she should survive me, that then my younger son, William, shall keep, maintain and support her during her natural life.

Also I give and bequeath unto my son, Michael, of the Parish of Hadlow in the County of Kent, the sum of five pounds of lawful money and also I give and bequeath unto my son, John, of the Parish of Rotherfield in the County of Sussex, the sum of five pounds.

And moreover, I give and bequeath unto my son, Richard, of the Parish of Framfield in the said County of Sussex, the further sum of five pounds, to them and their heirs forever absolutely.

And also I give and bequeath unto my five daughters the sum of five pounds (that is to say) unto my daughter, Mary Dadswell, the sum of one pound and to my daughter, Elizabeth Clark, the sum of one pound and to my daughter, Sarah Humphrey, the sum of one pound and my daughter, Ann Goodwin, the sum of one pound and my daughter, Barbara Newnham, the sum of one pound, to them and their heirs forever, absolutely. And my will and meaning is that all the before mentioned legacies be paid at the expiration of six months after my decease by my said Executors hereinafter mentioned unless it should so happen that any one or more of my said daughters should decease before me, then my will and meaning is that her or their part be equally shared or divided among my other daughters that are then and at that time, living.

And lastly, I give and bequeath unto my youngest son, William, of the Parish of Framfield in the County of Sussex, Labourer, all the rest, residue or remainder of my personal estate of what kind or quality soever may be to him and to his heirs forever lawfully begotten of his own body, that is to say one copyhold messuage or tenement together with one acre and a half of land holden of the Manor of Framfield, situate lying and being on Lancaster Wood in the Parish of Framfield, together with all outbuildings thereon standing or erected or may be erected at any time thereafter during my natural life.

Also I give and bequeath unto him, my son William, all my household furniture of all kinds whatsoever, together with all husbandry tackle or working tools and working implements of all sorts, also all live and dead stock of what kind or quality may be, as also all money and money worth and all securities for money that I do at this time possess or may possess at any time hereafter during my natural life to him and his heirs forever absolutely unless it should happen that my son, William, should decease not leaving any male heirs lawfully begotten of his own body then I give and bequeath the before mentioned copyhold messuage or tenement together with the one and a half acres of land, to my son Richard, to him and his heirs male forever absolutely. And if his heirs male should decease not leaving any male heir, then my will and meaning is that the next male heir at law, by the name of Brooker, shall inherit and enjoy the premises and to him and his heirs male forever that the said messuage or tenement may never go out of the name of Brooker………………..

 

 

 

Notes:

A Messuage or Tenement is a term used in the Manor Court Books to indicate a property, in this case a Cottage at Blackboys in the Parish of Framfield.

No punctuation is ever used in Wills but punctuation has been added here to ease the reading of the document. Otherwise it is just a long string of words.

William, Michael’s son, held the cottage for a number of years but since he never married, the cottage passed eventually to his brother, Richard, in accordance with the terms of the Will. Unfortunately, Richard’s son (Richard Junior) did sell the property on in the late 19th century, but he may not have known about the note in the Will of his Grandfather, saying that the property should never go out of the name of Brooker.
