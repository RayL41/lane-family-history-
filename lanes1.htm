• THE LANE FAMILY.

    Shravokee

    County Clare – Ireland

    •

    • No event in Irish history had a more emotional effect on Irish national feeling than the Great Famine of 1845-1849.

    The continual disastrous failure of the potato crop, due to a killer fungus, had by February, 1846 struck every county in Ireland and destroyed three-quarters of the country's crop.

    Conditions in Ireland up until that time were poor and travelers often commented on the extreme misery of the poorest Irish peasant. He lived off a tiny piece of land for which he paid such a high rent that almost all, and sometimes all, the cereal crops he grew on it had to be sold to pay the rent, with the result that the farmer and his family subsisted on a plot of potatoes which became the staple diet for the people of Ireland.

    This situation had become increasingly precarious during the first four decades of the nineteenth century, which had seen a vast population explosion in Ireland. The population of Ireland in 1800 was estimated at 4.5 million however this had increased to 8 million by 1841.

    The pressure of this vast increase of people on the land became a serious problem as land became subdivided into smaller and smaller plots, until half of the farms were less than 3 acres in size, on which increasingly people subsisted on potatoes, the major crop.

    The crop failed due to the disease for four successive years.

    Deaths from starvation in Ireland during the famine years 1845-1849 have been estimated at approximately one million people.

    In County Galway, it was reported that there were dead bodies everywhere and that every village had bodies lying unburied for many days. The streets were daily thronged with moving skeletons and the fields were strewn with the dead. Food riots were occurring in every County due to the population starving. Typhus was soon raging throughout Ireland resulting in many deaths.

    It was in this context that mass emigration occurred with people leaving from every port in Ireland. In 1847, some 250,000 people left Ireland and the rate was to continue at that level and sometimes higher for the next four years. Many of them traveled to the United States, Canada and also Australia.

    The Lane family came from Clonlara, County Clare in the south west of Ireland, a few miles north of Limerick City overlooking the River Shannon. Clonlara was once known as Doonass and many places in the village and surrounds including the Falls of Doonass are still called by this name.

    Doonass or Dun Easa means the Fort of the Rapids. In 1841, the population of the rural districts was 4016 with 629 houses.

    Various newspaper obituaries of the early Lane families in Australia confirm that they came from Shravokee in the Parish of Doonass in County Clare.

    The whole of Ireland was ruled by the English in those days and had a number of unusual terminology’s relating to the areas of the country and of course, many of the people used the old Catholic Church Terminology.

    •
      • The townland is the smallest and most ancient of the Irish land divisions. There were 60,462 townlands in Ireland. Shravokee was the old local name for the official townland of Srawickeen..
      • The Civil Parish is in effect a group of Townlands, which in our case was Kiltenanlea.
      • The Roman Catholic Parish was Donnas. Due to the imposition of the English System of Local Government in the 16th Century, Ireland had to adapt to a different Civil Parish system centered on towns and villages. The local people continued to use the Roman Catholic system.
      • The Poor Law Union – Ireland in 1838 was divided into unions or districts to which the local ratable tenants were to be financially responsible for the care of paupers or poor people in their areas. These areas were generally centered on a large market town. In this case, the Poor Law Union was Limerick.
      • Barony – There were 331 baronies in Ireland, which tended to reflect the old holdings of the Irish clans and the barony in this case was the Barony of Tulla Lower.
      • Ireland was divided into four provinces and Clonlara was in the province of Munster.
    • The circumstances of the Lane Family before and during the famine period are not known, however we do know from the Griffith Valuations of Rateable Property in Ireland of 1851 that various Lanes’ were farming at that time in the Parish Of Kiltenanlea. We also know from Michael Lane’s obituary that his father wanted to settle him on a farm but he came to Australia instead, leaving us to think they were in reasonable circumstances.

      Currently we can trace our Lane family back to Michael Lane, b1788, who married Catherine Moloney and they had at least three children, James and Patrick both born about 1811 who both came to Australia and Thomas, b1818, from whom we are descended and who stayed in Ireland.

      Thomas Lane married Elizabeth (Betty) Gleeson, b1820, of the Glen, Killaloe, Co. Clare. She had a brother who was a priest, and she also brought Peggy Moloney, who was blind and a cousin of her husband Thomas Lane to Shravokee, from what is now Hefferman’s near Larkin’s Pub (at crossroads to Parteen/Garraun) on the Limerick/Broadford Road.

      Thomas Lane and Elizabeth Gleeson had four boys -:

      Michael, b1838, who came to Australia and married Ellen Moloney, b1840. She was a sister of Roderick (Roddy) Moloney. Her parents Michael Moloney and Ellen Bolton had nine children and are buried at Teampuill Cemetry near Shravokee. We are descended from Michael Lane and Ellen Moloney.

      Thomas, b1839, who remained on the home farm at Shravokee, married Mary Ward b1843, of Annaholty, Birdhill, Co. Tipperary and had seven children.

      Thomas, b. Jan 13th 1874, d. February 25th 1908. He was a grocers assistant.

      James, b March 14th 1877, d. September 6th 1894 at the early age of 17years.

      John, b. May 2nd 1879 d. March 1934. He married Ellen McMahon of Redgate, Nr. Limerick in February 1923.

      Michael, b. Nov 12th 1881, d. August 2th 1915 of Consumption. He was a bachelor farmer.

      Mary, also known as Babe, remained on the farm and died in 1947 at the age of 71 years.

      William, b. January 12th 1884 and died in 1954. He emigrated to the USA and married a Miss Spaight of Derryfadda, Clonlara, Co. Clare. They had a daughter Margaret who resided at 232 Warren Street, Brooklyn, New York.

      Margaret – no details available.

      James, b1844, became a priest. He entered Mount Melleray and from there he went in 1865 to All Hallows College, Dublin. He was destined for the Maitland Diocese in Australia, but was finally ordained on 26th September, 1870 in the Cathedral Church of Bell Bay, USA. He served in that diocese until his death on the 25th January, 1877.

      Patrick, b1849, had a Butcher’s Shop at 8 Roches Street, Limerick and a farm at Shravokee. This is now Buckley’s farm. He married Mary Lynch of Co. Limerick and appears to have died as a result of an accident, slaughtering cattle. They were to have four children.

      Agnes who emigrated to the USA and married a Mr. Neville of Co. Limerick. There were two children of this marriage – one was killed in the war and the other is now married and lives/lived in Chicago.

      Kathleen lived at 4 Killuran Terrace, Catherine Street, Limerick and married a Mr. Coleman. She died in 1958 and supplied a lot of this Irish family history. She had no family but one of her brother Tom’s children, Joe lived with her. He worked at Boyds of William Street up to about 1956.

      Tom, married and had at least two children, Joe and Angela.

      Michael, no detail known.

      We know James Lane, who arrived in Australia in 1841, on his death certificate had listed, as his parents, Michael Lane and Catherine Moloney and also that Michael Lane, senior, advised that James was his uncle.

      We also know that the parents of Michael Lane, senior, were Thomas Lane and Elizabeth Gleeson and that he had a brother Thomas and another brother James a priest.

      Many of the Irish immigrants who arrived in Victoria in the mid 1800’s settled in the Western District of Victoria around Koroit , Tower Hill, Killarney, Crossley and Warrnambool as the farming conditions were very similar to Ireland.

      The various Lane’s from Shravokee, which is a very small townland in County Clare, are very prominent around this area and they all came out to Australia about the same time and were obviously related. There were also families of Moloney’s, Gleeson’s, Malone and Maddens who settled from the same area.

      Early arrivals of the Lane’s to Victoria were -:

      1. James Lane, born approximately 1811, at County Clare, Ireland, the son of Michael Lane and Catherine Moloney is the first known of our Lane family to arrive in Victoria. He arrived on the "Neptune" on March 29th, 1841. He was according, to the shipping records 25 years of age, and was accompanied by his brother, Patrick Lane aged 27 years. Both could read and write and their occupations were listed as laborers. It is interesting to note that in 1841, 61% of all the people in Ireland were illiterate. A bounty of 19 Pounds was paid to a Mr. John Marshall.
      2. The population of Victoria, or Port Phillip, as it was known was 20,416 in 1841, which had almost doubled from the previous year’s figure of 10,291. In 1841 almost 8000 assisted immigrants, and 543 unassisted immigrants arrived in the colony.

        Patrick Lane died on 1st October 1876 aged 65 years, an unmarried laborer. His Death Certificate states his parents were Michael Lane and Catherine Moloney of County Clare and he arrived in 1841. Cause of death was sudden due to congestion of the lungs, heart, stomach and intestines accelerated by drink and excitement.

        He died at Birregurra, which is a farming community about 80 miles from Warrnambool and is buried at Irrewarra Cemetery.

        The Informants were Michael McGrath and Martin Duck

        James died of exhaustion, a bachelor at the age of 76 on April 21st, 1887 at Koroit, Victoria.

        The informant of James’ death was Michael Lane, Senior, who indicated James was his uncle, and a witness at the burial, which was conducted at the Tower Hill cemetery near Koroit, was Roger Lane.

        A headstone at the Tower Hill cemetery carries the following inscription.

        Erected by Michael Lane - In memory of his Uncle James Lane

        Who died April 21st, 1887 aged 76 years.

        Also Michael Lane who died October 18th, 1912 aged 76 years.

        Also Ellen Lane, Relict of the above died 16th October 1921 aged 81 years.

        Bridget Lane Died 11th October 1950.

        Mary Ann Lane, Died 1958.

        Margaret Lane, Died 1958.

      3. Also on the Neptune was Jeremiah Maloney b1818, son of Patrick Moloney who was James Lane’s cousin, and who was to own Fala Park later to be farmed by Michael Lane. Jeremiah was accompanied by his brother Thomas, aged 15 and they came from Aharinagh, Kilmore, in Killokennedy Parish, County Clare, which is very close to Clonlara or Doonass.
      4. Jeremiah after his arrival married Margaret Doherty at Port Fairy on November 1st,1844. She was born at Kilfoy, County Clare in 1815.

        Jeremiah Maloney died on the 12th July, 1865, and left an annuity of 25 Pounds to his cousin James Lane so as long as he lived in Victoria.

      5. Thomas Lane who was born approximately 1833, the son of Thomas Lane and Ellen Moloney, was another early arrival in Victoria. He arrived in 1854, which was three years after the Port Phillip District became the Colony of Victoria.
      6. According to his obituary, "There died at The Survey, Killarney,

        yesterday morning an old resident in the person of Mr. Thomas Lane. Deceased was a native of County Clare, arriving in Victoria in 1854, when he went to the gold diggings where he remained some time with varying fortune. He was afterwards farming for some 13 years in the Warrnambool district and then settled at Killarney where he followed the occupation of a farmer for the last 22 years. He was a colonist of 46 years standing and was 67 years of age at the time of death, and leaves a widow and two sons".

        He married Catherine O'Shannessy at Warrnambool, who was the youngest daughter of Daniel and Judith O’ Shannessy of Shravokee, County Clare, on January 4th, 1868. A witness at the marriage was Roger Lane.

        Thomas Lane died August 27th, 1900 at the "Survey", Killarney aged 67 years and left three children Thomas b.1869, Daniel b.1870 and Mary Catherine b, 1873. He also is buried at Tower Hill cemetery. The informant of his death was his son Thomas Lane of Killarney.

        The land at "The Survey" was purchased on July 27th, 1876 and the family came to live there on May 2nd,1878.

        His daughter Mary Catherine married John Joseph Gleeson. Their daughter Mary [May] married John Joseph Wilkinson who was Mayor of Koroit in 1940, 1941, 1948, 1949, 1962 and 1963.

        John Wilkinson was to purchase at a later date one of Michael Lane's Farms.

        The sons Of Thomas Lane, Thomas and Daniel may have been referred to in Michael Lane, Senior's, will as his nephews, the sons of my brother Thomas, to whom he left the sum of 200 pounds.

        They were coffin bearers at his wife Ellen’s funeral.

        However he did have a brother in Ireland, Thomas, who also had sons.

      7. Roger Lane was the son of Thomas Lane and Mary Moore and was born at County Clare, Ireland in 1840. He arrived in Victoria approximately 1860 and married Mary O'Donnell at Port Fairy approximately 1885. He had a brother John who was a farmer at Fernhill, County Limerick, at the time of Roger’s death in 1926.
      8. Roger Lane died on June 6th, 1926 at Koroit and left no children. The informant of his death was Thomas Lane, farmer of Tower Hill.

        He is buried at Tower Hill Cemetery.

        His will left many donations to various charities but the bulk of his estate was left to his wife and then divided between his brother John in Ireland and his niece Ellen Gorman in Victoria. She was daughter of Thomas Lane & Margaret Lane of Crossley. His obituary stated that Margaret Lane was a sister.

        His obituary in The Koroit Sentinal June 12th, 1926 stated -:

        A very old resident of Koroit in the person of Mr. Roger Lane died at his residence on June 6th, 1926. The late Mr. Lane was born at Shra Bokee (Shravokee),County Clare, Ireland and landed in Melbourne 66 years ago then being 21 years of age.

        After spending a short time in Melbourne, he came to this district where he has resided unbrokenly until the time of his death. He successfully engaged in farming pursuits being thrifty and hard working and acquired a small farm close to Koroit. He was married in Warrnambool some years after coming to the district and his wife survives him.

        Two or three of his brothers and one sister went to America. His brother Michael died at Koroit many years ago. The late Mrs. Thos. Lane of Crossley was a sister.

        The funeral took place on Wednesday morning and was largely attended. Many members of the Hibernian Society (of which he was a member) attended in regalia.

      9. Thomas Lane who was born c1833 the eldest son of John Lane and Mary Hogan of Co. Limerick, Ireland. He arrived in Victoria approximately 1851 and on 10th July, 1867 at Warrnambool, at the age of 34 married Margaret Lane, aged 21. She was the daughter of Thomas Lane and Ellen Moloney of Co. Clare, Ireland.
      10. Witnesses at the wedding were a Michael Lane and Mary Anne Lane.

        Thomas Lane died 13th July, 1905 and is buried at Tower Hill Cemetery.

        His obituary stated -:

        An old and respected colonist died at his residence, Crossley, on the 13th instant., in the person of Mr Thomas Lane, who arrived in Victoria about 54 years ago. He was identified with the early days of the gold diggings and 35 years ago he purchased land at Crossley where he was engaged in farming until his death which occurred at the age of 74years. He leaves a widow and grown up family to mourn their loss. The late Mr Lane was the eldest son of Mr John Lane of Firhill, County Limerick, Ireland and was very highly respected throughout the distict. The funeral took place last saturday, where considerably over 100 vehicles followed the remains to their last resting place, the procession being a mile and a half in length. The Rev. P. Lennon conducted the funeral service at the grave.

      11. Margaret Lane, wife of Thomas Lane, was born c. 1846 the daughter of Thomas Lane and Ellen Moloney, of Co. Clare, Ireland and she arrived in Victoria c. 1867 the same year she was married at age 21. She died at age 75 on 10th June, 1921. The informant on her death Certificate was her son Thomas Lane Of Illowa and her death certificate stated her parents were Thomas Lane and Mary Moloney and that she was born at Co. Clare. The Christian name of her mother was possibly an error as Margaret stated on her wedding certificate which she signed that her mother was Ellen Moloney.
  • Margaret Lane is also buried at Tower Hill.
