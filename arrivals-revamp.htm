Between 1845 and 1849, during Ireland's great famine, more than one million people died from starvation and disease. The famine drove many people to emigrate, seeking a new life in Australia, the United States or Canada. 

Victoria, then part of New South Wales, had a growing demand for skilled workers, particularly farm labourers. The government's bounty system encouraged immigration to meet this need.

Letters home from Irish settlers in Victoria encouraged relatives and friends to follow. Work was plentiful, offering the chance to save for land of their own. In Ireland, many farmers leased their land and had little opportunity to become landholders.

As large pastoral runs in Victoria were divided into smaller holdings, agricultural land became available for purchase.

The rich volcanic soils around Belfast (now Port Fairy) and Tower Hill attracted many young Irish settlers. James Atkinson's 5120 acres and William Rutledge's 5120 acres were divided into smaller allotments, creating opportunities to buy farmland. Rutledge's special survey, Farnham Park, taken up 1841 was sold at £1  an acre when it was broken up.

 

James, Patrick and John Lane came to Victoria as young, unmarried men from Shravokee, a small Townland in County Clare, Ireland. This was when Victoria was still part of New South Wales.
John later married Sarah Darcy, while James and Patrick remained unmarried.


Their brother Thomas Lane remained in Ireland and as the oldest son inherited the family farm. His son Michael Lane followed his uncles to Victoria in 1861 and became a successful farmer.

During the 1840s and 1850s other Lane families and relatives including the Moloneys, Gleesons, Malones and Maddens also came to Victoria from the same area in County Clare. They were attracted by familiar farming conditions and opportunities created by the discovery of gold.

Grandson: Michael Lane (born 1841) son of Thomas Lane (born 1818) married Ellen Moloney

Michael Lane, born 1788, of Shravokee, Clonlara, County Clare married Catherine Moloney. They had at least four children, James (born 1816), Patrick (born 1817) , Thomas (born 1818) and John (born 1825). James, Patrick, and John emigrated to Australia. Thomas (born 1818), from whom we are descended, remained in Ireland.

Michael Lane, (born 1841) and Ellen Moloney. He was the son of Thomas Lane and Elizabeth Gleeson and grandson of Michael Lane and Catherine Moloney, emigrated from Shravokee, County Clare, aboard the Great Victoria. The ship departed Liverpool in September 1864 and arrived at Williamstown in November 1864. Travelling with him were his future wife, Ellen Moloney, and her sister Mary. Michael and Ellen married at St. Patrick's Church, Belfast-now Port Fairy-on 1 June 1870.
Michael became a successful Western District farmer, farming near Warrnambool, Koroit and Yangery. He served for many years on the Koroit Borough Council and was elected Mayor in 1910. Michael and Ellen established the Victorian Branch from which our direct family line descends. (Refer the full story of Michael Lane and Ellen Moloney) 

James Lane and Patrick Lane were the earliest  known members of our Lane family to arrive in Victoria.  They arrived in Victoria on the "Neptune"  on 29th March, 1841, having departed Plymouth, England on the 12th December, 1840. Shipping records give their ages as 25 and 26 years of age respectively. Both could read and write and their occupations were listed as laborers. A bounty of £19 was paid to a Mr. John Marshall.

Also on the shipping list were their cousins Jeremiah and Thomas Maloney. James Lane and Patrick Lane's mother was Catherine Moloney.

James Lane died of exhaustion at Koroit, Victoria, on 21st April, 1887 aged 76. He remained unmarried.
His Death Certificate lists his parents as Michael Lane, a farmer and Catherine Lane, formerly Moloney.

Michael Lane, reported the death and identified James as his uncle (see below) James was buried at the Tower Hill Cemetery, near Koroit, with Roger Lane recorded as a witness.

The headstone at the Tower Hill Cemetery bears the following inscription.

  Erected by Michael Lane 

 In memory of his Uncle, James Lane

Who died April 21st, 1887 aged 76 years. 

Also Michael Lane who died October 18th, 1912 aged 76 years. 

Also Ellen Lane, Relict of the above died 16th October 1921 aged 81 years. 

Bridget Lane Died 11th October 1950.

Mary Ann Lane, Died 1958.

Margaret Lane, Died 1958.

Headstone of Michael Lane

James Lane inherited £25 a year for life from his cousin Jeremiah Moloney, provided he remained in Victoria. They had travelled from Ireland on the same ship.
Jeremiah Moloney died on 12 July 1865.

  Patrick Lane's death certificate records the following:

Patrick Lane died on 1st October 1876, aged 65. He was unmarried and had worked as a labourer.
His parents were recorded as Michael Lane and Catherine Moloney of County Clare, Ireland. 
Patrick died suddenly. His death certificate recorded congestion of the lungs, heart, stomach and intestines accelerated by drink and excitement. 
He died at Birregurra, a small farming community about 80 miles from Warrnambool and was buried at Irrewarra Cemetery. 
The informants were Michael McGrath and Martin Duck.

An inquest was held on Patrick’s death and his brother John Lane gave evidence.

 John Lane, born 1825 in County Clare, Ireland, married Sarah Darcy, at Warrnambool on 5th October, 1853.
Sarah was 18 and had also been born in County Clare. John gave his age as 28 and named   his parents as Michael Lane, a farmer and Catherine Maloney.
Sarah’s parents were shown as Anthony Darcy and Annoria Leary.

John and Sarah appear to have settled in Sandhurst about 1854. They spent at least 7 years there before moving to Birregurra.

Gold was found at Sandhurst in late 1851.In 1891 the town became known as Bendigo.

The Pioneer Index records Sarah's death in 1870. She was recorded as aged 38, born in County Clare with her parents listed as Anthony and Honoria Darcy. 
The Pioneer Index records the birth of her son James Lane at Birregurra in 1871. He died that same year aged eight months.
 

John died on August 13th, 1898, at Birregurra. His age was recorded as 90, although his stated birth year of 1825 suggests he was about 73. The informant, C. J. Barry who was present when John died, incorrectly recorded his mother's name as Gorman..
The certificate records his occupation as a laborer and states he had spent 47 years in the Australian Colonies mostly in Victoria. He was buried at Irrewarra Cemetery.

His children were listed as Michael and Luke (both deceased), Thomas (43), John (36), Anthony (34) and Patrick (32) 

Anthony was not listed on his mother's death certificate and no birth index entry has been found for him.

Anthony Lane died at Redfern, NSW in 1937 and his brother Thomas Lane at Woollahra, NSW, in 1939. 
 

His obituary in the Colac Herald, published on 16 August, 1898 stated:

Death has removed another old and respected resident ot the district in the person of Mr. John Lane, which took place at his residence, Birregurra, on Saturday morning last, the cause of death being pleurisy. He has been a resident of the district for more than 30 years and in the colony for 60 years. The deceased who had reached the grand old age of 90 years leaves a family of 4 sons, 3 of whom reside in NSW and 1 in Camperdown. His remains were interred at Warncoort cemetery on Sunday, a large number of vehicles and horsemen following the cortège to the last resting place.

His will dated 12th August, 1898 left his estate valued at £29 to his eldest son Thomas Lane of 125 Queen Street, Woollahra, Sydney, NSW..

John and Sarah Lane had seven children, three of whom died in childhood. They had settled in in Sandhurst (Bendigo) prior to 1854 where they spent at least seven years before settling at Birregurra.

Roger Lane was born in County Clare, Ireland, in 1847, the son of Thomas Lane and Ellen Maloney. He was born into a large family and many of his siblings emmigrated to Australia and the USA. 

He arrived in Victoria in the 1860s and married on June 14th, 1877, at Port Fairy, His wife, Margaret O’Brien, was a widow, aged 27, born in County Clare. Her maiden name was O’Donnell. 
The witnesses were John Moloney and Mary O'Donnell. 

Roger Lane died on June 6th, 1926 at Koroit. He had no children.
The informant was Thomas Lane, farmer of Tower Hill. 

Roger's will, dated 1923, included a bequest to his brother John Lane, a farmer at Clonlara, County Clare, Ireland. John had died in 1919.
Solicitors also followed up descendants of Roger's brother Jeremiah Lane who had died in New York in 1890, in connection with the bequest.

Roger Lane was buried at Tower Hill Cemetery. Gravestone of Roger Lane.

His wife Margaret Lane died on 3 July, 1929.  Gravestone of Margaret Lane.

In his will  he made bequests to several charities but, most of his estate was left to his wife, Margaret Lane, nee O'Donnell. On her death it was to be divided between his brother John Lane in Ireland and his niece Ellen Gorman in Victoria. 

Ellen was the daughter of Thomas Lane and Margaret Lane of Crossley.  Roger's obituary identified Margaret Lane of Crossley as his sister.

His obituary in The Koroit Sentinel,  published on 12 June, 1926 stated: 

"A very old resident of Koroit in the person of Mr. Roger Lane died at his residence on June 6th, 1926. The late Mr. Lane was born at Shravokee, County Clare, Ireland and landed in Melbourne 66 years ago then being 21 years of age. 

After spending a short time in Melbourne, he came to this district where he has resided unbrokenly until the time of his death.  He successfully engaged in farming pursuits being thrifty and hard working and acquired a small farm close to Koroit. He was married in Warrnambool some years after coming to the district and his wife survives him. 

Two or three of his brothers and one sister went to America. His brother Michael died at Koroit many years ago. The late Mrs. Thos. Lane of Crossley was a sister. 

The funeral took place on Wednesday morning and was largely attended. Many members of the Hibernian Society (of which he was a member) attended in regalia.

Roger Lane had a brother, James T. Lane  who emigrated from Shravokee, County Clare, Ireland, to New York in 1865 where he became very successful. He was a newspaper editor and reportedly a colonel during that the American Civil War although his military service remains unconfirmed
His obituary was published in the Koroit Sentinel on 27 June, 1908.
His reported Civil War service needs to be checked against his birth date and his arrival into the USA in 1865.

Note: His parents emmigrated to the USA as well as brothers Patrick (born 1835, Jeremiah (born 1852) and James T (born 1842)

Thomas Lane was born about 1833 at Shravokee, County Clare, Ireland. He arrived in Victoria arround 1854  aged about 21.
He married Catherine O'Shannessy at Warrnambool on 4 January, 1868.  Catherine was the youngest daughter of Daniel and Judith O’Shannessy of Shravokee, County Clare, Ireland. Her three brothers were James, John and Daniel. She arrived in Australia in 1852 aged 18.

Roger Lane (see above) witnessed the marriage.  Thomas’s marriage certificate dated 4 Jan 1868, which he signed, records him as a farmer, aged 35, born in County Clare and that his parents were Thomas Lane, a farmer, and Ellen Moloney. 

Thomas Lane farmed at Hopkins River, Warrnambool, and later purchased land at the "Survey" on the 27 July, 1876 and moved there on 2 May, 1878. This land formed part of William Rutledge's estate, which was subdivided following his death in 1876.

William Rutledge and Farnham Park.

William Rutledge was a prominent Sydney businessman born in Ireland who, with four partners, was granted 5,120 acres (2072 ha.) in 1843 under the special Survey System which was offered by the Colonial Office and which permitted capitalists to purchase 5,120 acres at a cost of £1 per acre  in any part of NSW of which the Port Phillip District (Victoria) then formed part.  He later bought out his four partners.

He named his Special Survey near Koroit "Farnham" and established a tenant community there.

Many of his tenant farmers came from poor Irish families whose passage he paid during the famine.  He was said to have an explosive temper, but was considered a  kind and considerate landlord who furnished his tenants with rations, seed and farming implements. 

William Rutledge died in 1876, aged 70, and the Survey was divided between his two sons.

The estate's first subdivision took place in July 1876 when 1,300 acres of the western part of the Survey were subdivided and auctioned. This land extended from Gorman's Lane at Tower Hill to Survey Lane at the Belfast end of the Survey.

The blocks ranged from 3 to 70 acres. On 29 July 1876, a further 729 acres were sold at prices ranging from £37 to £60 per acre.

A further 800 acres were sold in May 1878. 

Among the 46 buyers of land in 1876 were Thomas Lane, John Malone, John Moloney, John Quirk, T. Roche, J.J. Madden, G. Gorman, Thomas Roche and T. Gleeson, all from Shravokee or nearby areas.

Thomas Lane died at the "Survey", Killarney, on 27 August 1900, aged 67 years. He was buried at Tower Hill cemetery.  His Death Certificate dated 27 August 1900 names his parents as Thomas Lane and Ellen Maloney.  His son, Thomas, provided these details. Gravestone Of Thomas & Catherine Lane.

According to his obituary, "There died at The Survey, Killarney, yesterday morning an old resident in the person of Mr. Thomas Lane. Deceased was a native of County Clare, arriving in Victoria in 1854, when he went to the gold diggings with varying fortune. He was afterwards farming for some 13 years in the Warrnambool district and settled at Killarney where he followed the occupation of a farmer for the last 22 years. He was a colonist of 46 years standing and was 67 years of age at the time of death, and leaves a widow and two sons.

 Mary Ann Lane (born 1843) and Margaret Lane (born 1848) were also early arrivals from County Clare to the Colony of Victoria.

They appear to have arrived unaccompanied in their early twenties.
No record has been found identifying the ship on which they travelled.


They were the daughters of James Lane and Ann Malone of Shravokee, County Clare. They married two Gleeson brothers also from County Clare, whose parents were Matthew Gleeson and Bridget Rahilly. .

Mary Ann Lane, who was living at Woodford, married Thomas Gleeson, a farmer of Yangery, on 16 August 1866, when she was 23 years of age.
Witnesses at the wedding were James Moloney and Margaret Lane.
She named her parents as James Lane and Ann Malone.


She was aged 35 and was buried at Tower Hill Cemetery.

Son: Thomas Lane (born 1833) married Margaret Lane daughter of Thomas Lane and Ellen Moloney.

 

Thomas Lane was another early arrival. He was the eldest son of John Lane and Mary Hogan, of Firhill, Co. Limerick.   He married Margaret Lane, daughter of Thomas Lane and Ellen Maloney, of County Clare, on 14 July, 1867 at St. Joseph's Church, Warrnambool, Victoria.
 Thomas was 34 years old and a farmer at Tower Hill and Margaret was 21 years old and a farmer's daughter. The witnesses at the wedding were Michael Lane and Mary Anne Lane. 

Children of Thomas Lane and Margaret Lane

Ellen Lane born 1870 m. Coleman Gorman 1912
Thomas (Digger) Lane born 1871 m. Margaret Moloney 1908
John (Tennyson) Lane  born 1874 m. Elizabeth Gleeson 1909
Catherine Lane born 1876 m. John Gillick 1900

Thomas Lane died at Killarney. His death certificate recorded his age as 80, while his obituary gave it as 74.

His obituary stated:

An old and respected colonist died at his residence, Crossley, on the 13th inst., in the person of Mr. Thomas Lane, who arrived in Victoria about 54 years ago. He was identified with the early days of the gold diggings and 35 years ago he purchased land at Crossley, where he was engaged in farming until the time of his death, which occurred at the age of 74 years. He leaves a widow and grown up family to mourn their loss. The late Mr. Lane was the eldest son of Mr. John Lane of Firhill, Co. Limerick, Ireland and was very highly respected throughout the district. The funeral took place last Saturday, when considerably over 100 vehicles followed the remains to their last resting place, the procession being about a mile and a half in length. The Rev. P. Lennon conducted the service at the grave.

Note: The obituary reference to  Firhill, County Limerick, is believed to mean Firhill, County Clare, which is just over the county border.  

We know from his marriage certificate Thomas Lane was 34 years old in 1867 and therefore was born c. 1833.
His obituary gave his age as 74 years when he died in 1905, suggesting was born about 1831.
His death certificate recorded his age as 80, suggesting he was born about 1825..
His obituary stated that he had spent about 54 years in Victoria, suggesting he arrived about 1851. 

Her death certificate records that Margaret Lane died on 10 June 1921, aged 75, at the home of her daughter, Mrs. Coleman Gorman, in Crossley.  (See Roger Lane's Will of 1926).  The informant was her son Thomas Lane of Illowa, Victoria, who named her parents as  Thomas Lane and Mary Moloney and stated that she had lived 55 years in Victoria.  (It is possible that Ellen Moloney and Mary Moloney may have been the same person – Mary Ellen being a common name in Ireland at this time).

This would make Margaret a sister to Thomas Lane (born 1833) and Roger Lane (born 1847)  (see above).

Link to Thomas Lane's father, John Lane of Firhill, County Clare, Ireland. Thomas Lane's brother Michael Lane and his wife Joanna O'Grady were the centre of the famed Ardnacrusha Eviction at their Firhil house in September 1887 where they and their ten Children were savagely evicted from their 32 acre tenant farm where they had lived for 14 years and where John Lane had farmed for over 30 years. This episode in the Irish Land Wars made the Irish and International newspaper. eg. New York Times and London Evening Standard.  

Their daughter Ellen Lane married Coleman Francis Russell Gorman.
